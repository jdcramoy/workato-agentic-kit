# {{OWNER}}'s Writing Voice

Apply to any prose {{OWNER}} sends or publishes as themself. Use the tone, flow,
punctuation, and structure only; never carry over subject matter from source
samples.

HOW TO BUILD THIS FILE (one-time, ~30 minutes): collect 10-20 real sent emails,
Slack threads, and docs authored by {{OWNER}} across registers (peer technical,
exec, external, announcement). Read them side by side and write down what is
CONSTANT. Fill every section below from evidence, not aspiration. Re-mine twice
a year; voice drifts.

## Punctuation: the hard rules

Every writer has at least one mechanical tell. Mine the samples for punctuation
that is ALWAYS absent or ALWAYS present, and record it as a hard rule. Typical
candidates:

- Em/en dashes: used or never? If never, list the replacements (comma, colon,
  semicolon, parentheses) and when each applies.
- Bold/italics frequency: decoration or restraint?
- Contractions: always, or context-dependent?
- Emoji by channel: none in docs, real in chat? Real means genuine
  reaction emoji, never decoration.

## Register: who is the reader

State the default reader assumption (peer? exec? external?) and what that
implies. Record the two or three registers the writer actually uses and what
changes between them.

Never write (the anti-list): collect the AI-flavored or not-them phrases the
samples never contain ("I hope this email finds you", "delve", "It's worth
noting", framing that implies the reader is behind). This list is the
highest-value section: deletions are more reliable than insertions.

## Openers, closers, sign-off

How do real emails start (warm one-liners? straight to it?) and end (is the
signature block the sign-off, or is a typed name used?). Quote 2-3 real
openers per register as calibration anchors.

## Structure

Paragraph length, list habits (prose vs bullets vs tables), how instructions
are given (imperative? first person plural?), how disagreement or pushback is
phrased.

## Voice × people (the interaction)

This skill defines HOW you write. The people files
(`knowledge/people/<name>.md`) define HOW YOU ADAPT per recipient: shared
context, standing jokes, formality drift, what they've asked for before.
When drafting to a person WITH a people file, read both: this skill is the
base register, the people file is the per-recipient overlay. When drafting
to a person WITHOUT one, this skill alone applies.

## Privacy rules (load-bearing)

- This file is PERSONAL: it is you-as-a-writer. It lives in your local
  harness and is gitignored in any repo that others can see.
- Never copy subject matter, opinions about people, or private context from
  the source samples into this file. Voice only.
- Voice-rep practice drafts (drafts to real people, never sent) live
  separately and are also private.
